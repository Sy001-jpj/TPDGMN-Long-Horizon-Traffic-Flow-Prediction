TPDGMN: Tree-Prior Dynamic Graph Mamba Network for Efficient Long-Horizon Traffic Flow Prediction
Paper DOI:https://doi.org/10.1145/3821244.3821355

Multi-step traffic flow prediction is a key task in intelligent transportation systems (ITS), aiming to model both long-range dependencies and dynamic spatial correlations in complex and time-varying road networks. However, existing methods often struggle to balance dynamic characterization, robustness, and computational efficiency.To address this, we propose the Tree-Prior Dynamic Graph Mamba Network (TPDGMN) for multi-step traffic flow prediction.This model takes AGCRN as the baseline and replaces the recursive time encoder RNN with a Mamba encoder based on a selective state space model, reducing the serial computational cost under long historical windows and improving training efficiency while maintaining expressive power.Additionally, a dynamic graph learner is designed to adaptively generate time-varying adjacency relations from the data to capture variable spatial dependencies. Further, a tree structure prior regularization term is introduced to structurally constrain the learned adjacency matrix, promoting a sparser and more stable graph structure. We conducted extensive experiments on the traffic dataset from PeMS. The experimental results show that TPDGMN outperforms multiple benchmark methods in terms of MAE and RMSE. Moreover, in long sequence prediction tasks, TPDGMN significantly reduces training time, verifying the improvements in accuracy, robustness, and efficiency of the proposed method.
